## 2. Detailed Notes on Key Residuals

### 2.1 Unkeyed SHA-256 Audit Chain (`RGT-416` -> `N4`)
`go/internal/audit` and `go/internal/store` faithfully reproduce Python's audit chain format (`prev|timestamp|sanitize(action)|secret_name|sanitize(details)|policy_hash` under plain SHA-256). As verified in `TestAuditChainTamperDetectionAndUnkeyedLimitation`:
- Accidental corruption, single-field edits to hashed columns, and middle-row deletions are detected by `VerifyAuditChain`.
- However, `agent_id` and `prev_hash` are not part of the hash input, tail truncation is undetectable without an external head anchor, and any principal with write access to `vault.db` can recompute every `entry_hash` from genesis because SHA-256 is unkeyed.
- Fixing this requires a keyed HMAC chain (`N4`), which changes the on-disk `audit_logs` format and is therefore held until Python EOL removal (`GZ`) per `RGT-489`. The v2 HMAC chain design is specified in `docs/architecture/logging-policy.md` §8.2.

### 2.2 `secret_name` and `agent_id` Sanitization Boundary (`RGT-415`)
- `audit.Sanitize` encodes `\r`, `\n`, and `\t` in `action` and `details`, while `store.LogAudit` passes `secret_name` and `agent_id` without escaping so hashes remain byte-identical to Python.
- Every external boundary (`server.PathMiddleware`, `handlers.GetSecret`, `auth.Store.Mint`, and `vault.Vault` methods `SetSecret`, `Lease`, `Fingerprint`, `ListSecrets`, `RevokeSecret`, `Simulate`) validates `name`, `namespace`, and `agent` through `go/internal/validation`, rejecting CR, LF, TAB, ANSI escapes, and NUL before `store` is reached (`TestVaultBoundaryRejectsHostileNamesAndAgentsBeforeAudit`).
- Operational log forwarding via `go/internal/logging` and `handlers.LogSafe` encodes control characters on output (`TestStoreAndVaultDirectAuditSanitizationAndFrozenFormatResidual`).
